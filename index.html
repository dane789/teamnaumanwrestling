FIND YOUR STARTING POINT

A fun start.
Room to grow.

Games, movement and wrestling fundamentals.
Find the right start for your child.

01AGES 4–10

Roots

A playful first step onto the mat. Give that big energy somewhere to go. Kids learn through tumbling, agility, body-weight exercises and wrestling games such as shoelace tag, flag tag and sumo. Along the way, they explore the rules and try foundational moves like a stance, shot, sprawl, half-nelson and stand-up. It’s an active introduction designed to make learning wrestling fun.

Ages 4–10 · Limited to 20 wrestlers · One or two one-hour sessions per week.

Brad Bechtel · Logan Stoltzfus · Dan Nauman

Roots registration form ↗
02AGES 6–12

Saplings

Keep the fun. Build the skills. For kids ready to grow beyond their first introduction, Saplings builds on wrestling fundamentals through gymnastics, body control and more technique. The emphasis stays on an enjoyable, active workout while children practice moving with purpose and putting their skills together. Roots graduates receive selection priority.

Ages 6–12 · Limited to 20 wrestlers · One one-hour session per week; extra practices may be added.

Confirm your assigned session and available places with the club.

Club training

Your next step starts in the room. Learn a technique, drill it with a partner and put it into action. Train alongside teammates while building the discipline, respect and steady effort at the heart of Team Nauman’s mission.

Find your training group with a coach, or ask about focused private and small-group lessons.

BUILD YOUR TECHNIQUE

Developmental Drillers

For wrestlers with roughly 2–3+ years of experience. Instruction, drilling and situations, followed by wrestling that puts the technique to work. Regular training rates apply.

Ask a coach about placement and the current session.

RAISE THE PACE

Advanced Assassins

For wrestlers with roughly 3–4+ years of experience. Faster instruction and high-level drilling, followed by live wrestling, drill matches or play wrestling. Regular training rates apply.

Ask a coach about placement and the current session.

MORE MAT TIME. MORE PURPOSE.

Come ready to learn. Leave with something to work on.

Regular practice is a chance to ask questions, repeat the details and test your technique with training partners. The newsletter describes developmental sessions with instruction, drilling and situations followed by wrestling; experienced groups move at a faster pace with advanced drilling and live or drill matches. A coach can help you find the right fit for your experience.

For wrestlers

Bring a goal: improve a finish, get comfortable in a position, or make a technique feel more natural. Work with teammates, learn from feedback and keep building through consistent practice.

For parents

Expect a room focused on technique and character, with discipline, respect, sportsmanship and teamwork at its core. Before the first visit, confirm the appropriate group, current session and membership requirements with a coach.

A coach in your corner.

Private or small-group lessons make room to focus on your wrestler’s questions and goals. A family might ask to revisit a move from practice, work through a difficult position or plan focused preparation for an upcoming event. Discuss the goal with the coach first so the session has a clear purpose.

Start with the coach biographies below. Getting ready for youth wrestling

Beginners are welcome. Wear shorts and a T-shirt; avoid jeans, zippers and jewelry. Wrestling shoes are recommended, but beginners can ask the coach about starting without them. Parents may watch from the designated area. Confirm current requirements before your first visit.
