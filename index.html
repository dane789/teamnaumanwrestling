START YOUNG

Learn the fundamentals.

Start with movement, games and the basics—then keep building. Roots (ages 4–10) introduces wrestling; Saplings (ages 6–12) develops body control and technique. Roots graduates receive selection priority.

Staying involved gives your wrestler time to revisit skills, learn from familiar coaches and grow with training partners. Regular practice helps turn the basics into habits, while coaches can get to know your child’s strengths and next steps.

Showing up, supporting teammates and celebrating each other’s progress builds trust, pride in the club and a team your family can belong to. Ask a coach about the right starting point and the path ahead.

FIND YOUR STARTING POINT

A fun start.
Room to grow.

01AGES 4–10

Roots

A playful first step onto the mat. Give that big energy somewhere to go. Kids learn through tumbling, agility, body-weight exercises and wrestling games such as shoelace tag, flag tag and sumo. Along the way, they explore the rules and try foundational moves like a stance, shot, sprawl, half-nelson and stand-up. It’s an active introduction designed to make learning wrestling fun.

Ages 4–10 · Limited to 20 wrestlers · One or two one-hour sessions per week.

Brad Bechtel · Logan Stoltzfus · Dan Nauman

Roots registration form ↗
02AGES 6–12

Saplings

Keep the fun. Build the skills. For kids ready to grow beyond their first introduction, Saplings builds on wrestling fundamentals through gymnastics, body control and more technique. The emphasis stays on an enjoyable, active workout while children practice moving with purpose and putting their skills together. Roots graduates receive selection priority.

Ages 6–12 · Limited to 20 wrestlers · One one-hour session per week; extra practices may be added.

Getting ready for youth wrestling

Beginners are welcome. Wear shorts and a T-shirt; avoid jeans, zippers and jewelry. Wrestling shoes are recommended, but beginners can ask the coach about starting without them. Parents may watch from the designated area. Confirm current requirements before your first visit.

Which practice is right for my wrestler?

Just getting started? Roots (ages 4–10) introduces wrestling through games and movement. Saplings (ages 6–12) builds body control and basic technique. Explore youth programs and registration.

Building technique: Developmental Drillers is described in the newsletter for wrestlers with roughly 2–3+ years of experience: about an hour of instruction, drilling and situations, followed by 30 minutes applying the technique in wrestling.

Ready for a faster pace: Advanced Assassins is described for roughly 3–4+ years of experience: 45–50 minutes of instruction and high-level drilling, then 40–45 minutes of live wrestling, drill matches or play wrestling. Coaches pause to correct and highlight details.

A coach confirms placement and which weekly session offers each group. The weekly “All ages” listing does not mean every experience group trains together.

THE TEAM NAUMAN MISSION

Build the wrestler.
Develop the person.

Help every athlete reach their potential—on the mat, in the classroom and in life.

Team Nauman’s mission brings expert coaching and personalized training together with discipline and respect. We strive to develop strength, technique and character, so wrestlers represent themselves, their families and their schools with integrity in competition and in the community.

01 / STRENGTH

Keep showing up.

Build the discipline to prepare, the perseverance to work through challenges and the resilience to keep learning. Progress grows through consistent effort.

02 / TECHNIQUE

Learn with purpose.

Work toward excellence through coaching, focused practice and attention to detail. Bring a growth mindset: ask questions, accept feedback and keep improving.

03 / CHARACTER

Carry it beyond the mat.

Act with honesty. Respect teammates, coaches and opponents. Compete with sportsmanship and represent your family, school and community with pride.

A team to grow with. A family to stand beside you.

Teamwork means supporting and uplifting one another. At TNWC, the mission asks wrestlers to pursue their own goals while helping teammates grow—celebrating effort, learning through setbacks and working toward shared success.
